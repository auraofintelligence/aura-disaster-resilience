# PHY-07 How will separate equipment spaces, drains, cable openings and power shut-offs keep a fault in one part from spreading to people or the essential information service?

[Start here](Home.md) · [Questions](Question-register.md) · [Progress](Progress.md) · [People decide](Human-review.md)

## Why this matters

Careful separation lets the design deal with a broken screen, water entry, battery fault or high-power computer failure.

## What to bring back

Draw the compartments and every opening between them. Show how each fault could spread, how power is made safe, where people are kept clear and how service returns. If electromagnetic shielding is included, define the protection to measure and the test needed.

Separate faults by design: keep public controls, stored energy, power conversion and information service in reviewed compartments. Map every opening and shared cable, provide labelled isolation, and ensure one failed module can be removed while an independent notice layer explains available service.

### Compartment and isolation drawing

**AI-generated adaptation of an existing pattern.** Have the electrical designer compare separate cabinets with protected internal sections, documenting fault paths and safe maintenance access. Use enclosure accessories only within their documented installation conditions and inspect the completed openings.

### Independent continuity cartridge

**AI-generated future concept.** Develop a small independently powered notice-and-status cartridge that remains readable when the main cabinet is isolated. A tested physical boundary would separate it from high-energy faults; a retained image would always carry its update time.
