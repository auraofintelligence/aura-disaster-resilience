# COM-07 How will people find information, ask for help and check whether a message arrived when mobile service, internet or their own phone is unavailable?

## Why this matters

Clear choices help people use the network with the equipment and assistance available to them.

## What to bring back

Draw and practise the journeys using the kiosk, local phone Wi-Fi, assisted service and paper. Show waiting, delivered and acknowledged messages, privacy and delivery limits. Test real phones and browsers, including one without 3D graphics support, and a person with no device. Explain that lodging a request and confirmed dispatch are separate steps.

### Accessible local information station

**AI-generated adaptation of an existing pattern.** Trial a local web service with simple phone pages and a public screen alongside printed updates. Compare available host internet and an agreed satellite option; have receiving services review help-request wording and delivery status.

Research basis: [D.D.A. guide: The ins and outs of access](https://www.humanrights.gov.au/our-work/disability-rights/dda-guide-ins-and-outs-access) · [How to prepare: nbn and emergency events](https://www.nbnco.com.au/corporate-information/about-nbn-co/newsroom/articles/community/how-to-prepare-nbn-and-emergency-events).

**AI-generated future concept.** Let a chosen personal Aura carry a minimal encrypted task between a phone, kiosk and assisted station. People without a profile or device would retain the same public information and a practical human help route.
