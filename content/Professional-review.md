# Initiative, professional review and better practice

[Luke Nathan Hayes](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/open-record-luke-nathan-hayes/tree/main) is the project initiator and will drive the work forward: research the possibilities, bring people together, prepare proposals and develop the kiosk and network. Community experience and qualified professional knowledge will help turn those proposals into dependable capability.

Where a proposal overlaps the responsibilities of the State Emergency Service (SES), police, fire and rescue, ambulance, the Australian Defence Force or other qualified professionals, Luke's opinions and research will be reviewed in that context. The review will compare the proposal with applicable Australian and international good practice, relevant professional procedures and current legal sources. It will record meaningful differences, reasonable improvements and deficiencies found in either the proposal or the existing approach.

## Match the review to the work

The people involved depend on the actual question: for example, electrical work, public warnings, a request for emergency assistance, a site evacuation, medical support or communications coordination. Identify the responsible service, the suitably qualified reviewer and their scope. Record their actual participation and authority when agreed. Agency names in a plan identify a connection to develop; appointments and partnerships are recorded when established.

Luke can initiate and coordinate design work and make choices within his own responsibilities. Relevant professionals assess the parts within their competence; authorised services and public bodies retain their operational and statutory decisions. Site owners, cultural custodians, participants and cooperative members retain their own decisions. [People decide](Human-review.md) explains how these roles fit together.

Routine authorised research, drafting and comparison can move forward while these relationships develop. The proposal names the particular action that needs a professional review, operating agreement or formal decision before proceeding.

## Compare the evidence in both directions

| Record | What a reader should be able to see |
| --- | --- |
| The proposal | The question, Luke's research or another contributor's recommendation, its intended benefit and the exact next action. |
| The applicable reference | The relevant law, procedure, standard or guidance; its issuer, edition, date, jurisdiction and the reason it applies to this task. Distinguish a binding requirement from guidance or a proposed approach. |
| The comparison | Where the proposal and reference agree, where they differ and which assumptions explain the difference. |
| Improvements and deficiencies | Potential benefits, gaps or weaknesses in the proposal and in the existing approach. State the supporting evidence, practical consequences and uncertainty. |
| The professional assessment | The reviewer's identity, competence, role, scope and actual comments, including any remaining disagreement. |
| The next useful step | Refine the design, obtain missing evidence, run a suitably authorised comparison or trial, or take a proposed change through the responsible decision process. |
| The decision | Who decided what, the exact edition and conditions, and the evidence needed for the next milestone. |

For example, a proposal might add a locally stored acknowledgement for a help request during a network outage. Compare it with the receiving service's actual process. Test whether it helps people understand the request's status, identify any misleading implication that help is on its way, and record the receiving service's assessment. The resulting design can improve the public experience while keeping receipt, acceptance and response decisions clear.

An improvement can come from practical local experience, Luke's research, a professional reviewer or a better-tested method elsewhere. Assess its evidence and consequences. A finding may identify a gap, a possible deficiency or a demonstrated improvement; state which has actually been established. Preserve reasonable disagreement and the response to it so the next design can build on the learning. Proposed changes affecting binding requirements follow the applicable formal process.

Use the [practice-comparison template](../templates/practice-comparison.md) for the working record. Link it to the question and proposal under review. This page establishes the review method; comparisons and professional findings are recorded when actually produced.

The [Council plan crosswalk](Council-plan-crosswalk.md) applies this preparation method to the supplied management and recovery plans. [Council plans and Aura integration](Council-integration.md) proposes service interfaces and a comparison path for demonstrating improvements. The [integration evidence template](../templates/council-integration-evidence.md) helps reviewers agree a baseline, inspect results and decide the next scoped action.

## Find the relevant practice and legal sources

These official routes help reviewers locate appropriate reference material. Each review identifies the particular edition and section it uses.

| Starting route | How it helps |
| --- | --- |
| [Australian Institute for Disaster Resilience: Emergency Planning handbook](https://knowledge.aidr.org.au/resources/handbook-emergency-planning/) | National good-practice principles for developing emergency plans, to apply alongside the actual operational arrangements. |
| [Queensland Disaster Management Guideline](https://www.disaster.qld.gov.au/disaster-management-guideline) | An official route to Queensland's disaster-management arrangements, responsibilities and related guidance. Check the issued version and relevant local arrangements. |
| [Queensland Standard for Disaster Management](https://www.igem.qld.gov.au/standard) | A local reference for shared responsibilities, outcomes, accountabilities and indicators, supporting different ways to achieve the required outcomes. |
| [ISO 22320: incident-management guidance](https://www.iso.org/standard/67851.html) | An international reference route for incident-management roles, responsibilities and cooperation. The catalogue identifies the standard; a review records the relevant provisions actually examined. |
| [UNDRR: Sendai Framework](https://www.undrr.org/implementing-sendai-framework/what-sendai-framework) | A wider international frame for risk understanding, governance, resilience investment and preparedness. |

[Standards Australia explains](https://www.standards.org.au/standards-development/what-is-standard) that standards are generally voluntary and can acquire mandatory effect through legal adoption. Establish the actual status and applicability of each reference for the work being reviewed.

The current legal explorations are [Your Relevance](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/australian-law-2012-lukes-relevance/pages/your-relevance.html), [P4A Legal RAG](https://p4a.xyz/pages/legal-rag.html) and [Australian Legal Engine](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/australian-legal-engine). Use them to help trace and examine source material. Record the underlying source, jurisdiction, operative date and any changes needed to establish current applicability. A historical corpus and a retrieved passage retain their dates; the responsible reviewer checks what applies to the present proposal.

The review compares the evidence and records the decision made by the relevant people. [Human review](Human-review.md), [evidence](Evidence.md) and [the source register](../docs/source-register.md) carry that trail into the next stage.
