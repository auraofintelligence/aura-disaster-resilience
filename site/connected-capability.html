    Installing the Missing Middle.

    Installing the Missing Middle proposes distributed community computing, useful recovered heat and a pathway into local ownership. Its 29 October 2026 development plan examines installation, commissioning, maintenance and hardware renewal as an ongoing business and workforce opportunity. [N1]

    The disaster relationship is an extension to investigate. Local services, place simulations and selected records might be supported across devices, community hubs, regional and remote systems. The plan itself establishes neither disaster deployment nor commissioned sites.

    Ask what keeps working when conditions change.

    Practical connections to investigate
    CapabilityResilience question
    Local computingWhich useful functions remain available when outside communications are lost?
    Recovered heatWhat assessed demand uses it, and what happens when compute or the heat recipient is unavailable?
    Uninterruptible power supply, or UPSWhich loads are protected, for what measured runtime, with what maintenance and longer-duration arrangements?
    Equipment renewalHow are repair, backup, restoration, training and replacement funded and maintained through each generation?

    A UPS does not establish indefinite operation. A heat-recovery arrangement retains appropriate alternative cooling and heating responses. Pumps, networking and controls belong in the assessment alongside the computers. Specific installation and engineering arrangements develop around each real site.

    Keep the work useful through generations.

    Building, plant and computing equipment have different lifecycles. The proposal examines upgrade capacity, renewal funding and suitable continued use for earlier equipment. A move to another task needs assessment; secondary sale and recycling remain other possible outcomes.

    Selected installation, commissioning and fault recordings could support maintained equipment records, just-in-time learning and paid mentoring. Recording permissions and qualified trade responsibilities travel with the work. Watching a lesson does not confer a licence. [N1] [N3]

    Shared assets and personal ownership have distinct homes.

    The proposed federation allows people and organisations to cooperate, with shared assets progressing into agreed local ownership. It does not require a compulsory parent company. Personal Auras and private archives remain personal; participation does not automatically transfer them into a civic twin.

    Voluntary stewardship keeps its C-Hour record. Paid trades, investment, operating finance and equipment ownership use their respective agreements. A C-Hour creates no monetary balance or shareholding.

    Expressions of interest help identify local readiness and resources. Physical deployment would follow actual site preparation, engineering, people and agreements. The national scale examples in the plan are planning assumptions, with no funding award or rollout timetable established.
