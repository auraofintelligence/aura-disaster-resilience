    Turn a broad intention into a useful conversation.

    Get Ready Queensland introduces understanding local risk, making a plan and preparing an emergency kit. Aura’s proposed contribution is to help someone connect that guidance with their circumstances and organise a manageable next step. Official information retains its source. [R3]

    A household may have a plan scattered through messages and paper. The first task might be checking contacts, finding the relevant local information or discussing who is usually home. Another household begins with pets, a support arrangement or a business on the property.

    A named contact becomes more useful when that person has agreed to the role. A provider explains its own continuity arrangements. A remembered possibility stays distinct from a confirmed service. Reminders and printed copies follow the tools actually available; dependable background notifications are further development work for the current personal browser tools. [P3]

    Respect the expertise people already use.

    Disability, ageing, care responsibilities and changing health needs bring practical knowledge about equipment, transport, communication and support. Inclusive preparedness begins with the person’s account of that knowledge. Get Ready Queensland’s person-centred emergency preparedness resources provide a relevant foundation. [R7]

    Someone may prefer plain text, captions, speech, a demonstration or time to discuss a choice. A chosen support person might join part of the conversation. The person reviews the account and decides what is shared. Support needs retain their context, rather than quietly becoming fixed AI assumptions.

    Children and teenagers take part through suitable age, supervision and safeguarding arrangements. Learning and public assistance remain accessible at the appropriate level. An early trial needs actual support roles and referral routes suited to its participants.

    Country and local knowledge shape the design.

    Minjerribah is Quandamooka Country. Local participation begins through actual relationships and the people with authority to speak for particular knowledge. Cultural custodians determine the conditions for use, interpretation, audience, copying and AI processing of their material. [P1] [R5]

    Community knowledge also includes how visitors arrive, who cares for shared places, which services people use and what maintenance really involves. A public source does not automatically grant permission to reconstruct a cultural account in a place twin or personal model.

    The National Principles for Disaster Recovery recognise community context, connections to Country and community-led decisions. The same respect belongs in preparation. [R8]

    Illustrative exercise · Sample people and messages

    Anna discovers an assumption.

    Anna lives with her teenage son and sometimes supports an older relative. In an exercise, her Aura helps gather earlier plans and prepare questions. A transport interruption reveals that she had treated a possible lift as an agreed arrangement.

    The useful result is a clearer question for the actual people involved. Anna also asks for a shorter explanation with a route back to the original source. That correction becomes part of how her Aura learns to assist her. The exercise demonstrates a learning opportunity, while real preparedness still depends on the arrangements being made.
